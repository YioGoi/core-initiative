01 / CONCEPT AND BRAND MAP

Core Initiative and six specialist brands

Core Initiative sits at the center of six boutique specialist brands.

Each brand has its own site and character. A shared origin, approach to making, and connected areas of expertise bring the family together. The names draw on The Matrix and Tool; the seven-circle relationship follows the Seed of Life idea.

The first goal is to reach people with small web and software needs in the Netherlands, while developing our own products and measuring visibility and enquiries.

02 / ART DIRECTION AND STYLE GUIDES

One family, distinct visual worlds

We want the confidence of an established business, the character of a boutique studio, and the energy of making things. Each site gets its own art direction, connected through a few recognizable family details.

Agreed visual direction

  • A separate style guide for each brand: fonts, colors, grids, spacing, surfaces, shadows, and motion.
  • Strong transitions and considered scroll narratives, especially for design and creative production. Motion should express the work and the idea behind the brand.
  • Construct: Blue-gray, structural, spatial, and masculine as an initial direction.
  • Cookie: Red moving toward pink; warm, caring, sensory, and feminine as an initial direction.

Proposed family standards

Design note: a direction board, one sample screen, and a small style guide can help evaluate the family resemblance early. The colors in this brief are presentation choices, not final palettes for the other brands.

03 / TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

Shared foundations behind the sites

Next.js brand sites, a shared ASP.NET Core backend, and a simple enquiry dashboard built with our own components. The initial goal is to keep incoming interest visible in one place.

Agreed decisions

  • Frontend: Next.js. Dutch by default, with English and Turkish versions.
  • Backend: ASP.NET Core. One application for shared form intake, records, and enquiry management.
  • Dashboard: Our own interface, with team sign-in, a table, record details, notes, and status tracking.
  • Infrastructure: No Supabase in the initial plan.

Application structure

One monorepo contains independent site applications and shared code in common/. Core and Construct starters are implemented. The planned backend can separate contact intake, enquiries, and email into modules. The database engine is open; PostgreSQL is a candidate.

This diagram shows the proposed operations flow. The backend, dashboard, database, and email service are not implemented yet. Node.js remains an option for specialized tools when needed.

04 / FORMS, EMAIL, AND THE DASHBOARD

From a message to a tracked enquiry

People can reach us through each brand. Forms feed a shared record; phone calls and direct emails can be entered manually in the first version.

Proposed form flow

  1. Collect name, email, and message; phone is optional. Include the originating brand, language, and source page.
  2. Validate on the server and apply basic spam controls. Persist the enquiry and the notification job.
  3. Confirm receipt after the record is saved. Send the notification and retry temporary email failures.

Handle duplicate submissions. If email delivery fails, the enquiry remains in the dashboard. Keep private keys on the server and validate the submitted brand against the configured brand registry.

Example statuses: New / Discussing / Proposal sent / Completed / Closed. Final status names and the point at which an enquiry becomes a project remain open.

Email setup

  • Brand addresses: construct@coreinitiative.nl, cookie@coreinitiative.nl, and similar addresses. A shared inbox is an initial option.
  • Form notifications: Send from our own verified address and use the visitor address as Reply-To. An acknowledgement email is an optional addition.
  • Human correspondence: We need to reply from the relevant brand address. The mailbox provider and automated sending service are still open.
The first dashboard is restricted to the team through authentication and authorization. Automatic matching between mailbox conversations and enquiry records can be added when needed.

05 / FROM PRODUCTION TO DISCOVERY

Products, tools, and visibility

Our own applications and useful small tools give this structure a place to produce real work. What we learn and the results we measure can feed the services we eventually offer clients.

Proposed product placement

  • Mind Palace: Can be showcased through Tool while keeping its own product identity.
  • AI Team: Can connect to Agent and demonstrate the idea of collaborating agents.
  • Spoon: A workshop for images, video, music, games, and creative web tools. HTML-to-image conversion is one idea to explore.

Product users, accounts, and data stay within their own boundaries. Shared operations services can be introduced where useful. Long-running image or video work may need a queue, background processing, file retention rules, and usage limits.

SEO and AEO approach

  • Each brand develops its own expertise, audience, and original content. Related projects and services link to one another where useful.
  • Crawlable content, page titles, canonical URLs, sitemaps, mobile performance, and correctly matched language versions form the shared technical foundation.
  • Links can support discovery and relevance signals. Connecting seven sites does not automatically produce a ranking advantage. [1]
  • SEO fundamentals also apply to Google AI search features. For AEO, focus on clear answers, reliable content, and understandable expertise. [2]

Google generally groups subdomains with the root domain for search-result diversity; we cannot assume seven separate positions for the same query. [1] Match language versions with reciprocal hreflang links. [3]

Measurement: track search visibility, visits, tool usage, and contact enquiries separately. A tool user and a potential service client may arrive with different needs.
